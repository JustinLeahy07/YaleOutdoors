Statement of Purpose

Yale Outdoors serves the Yale community with high-quality and safe trips to explore the outdoors and develop wilderness skills. Yale Outdoors creates a community for students to meet and learn from others who are interested in the outdoors. Yale Outdoors provides affordable wilderness skills training sessions and outdoors equipment for the Yale community. Above all, Yale Outdoors spreads the love for the outdoors and appreciation for nature with the rest of the Yale community.

Membership

At Yale Outdoors, we offer a space for people of all commitment levels to participate. The following is a list of membership status, requirements, and benefits.

Member

Requirements. General Members are those who subscribe to the YO mailing list.

Benefits. Members are welcomed to apply to and attend all YO trips.

Pnuckle

Pnuckling is the process of a member becoming a leader. This process is only open to undergraduates. All members interested in Pnuckling must apply at the beginning of any semester. Pnuckles are expected to fully engage in the Pnuckle programming. The requirements are:

  • Attend Pnuckle orientation and initiation
  • Co-lead 1 trip or event with Leaders
  • Attend at least 3 YO trips or events
  • Attend 2 monthly club-wide meetings
  • Attend Pnuckle bonding activities led by the Pnucklemaster

Benefits. Pnuckles get priority over members and non-members on trips.

Leader

Leaders are those who have successfully completed a semester of pnuckling. Leadership status lasts until the end of each academic year (even for Fall pnuckles). In order to maintain an active Leader status, Leaders must complete these requirements each semester:

  • Lead at least 2 trips or events
  • Attend 2 monthly club-wide meetings
  • Attend 2 bi-weekly board meetings

The Co-Coordinators conduct 2 leadership evaluations each semester. At the end of each semester, those who have not satisfied the requirements above lose their active Leader status. Leaders who lose active status may regain status in the following semester if they complete the requirements before the mid-semester evaluation.

Benefits.

  • Funding (via the Treasurers) to make trip proposals into real trips
  • Leaders’ retreat
  • Gear rental discount
  • Voting rights (for Board and amendment changes)

Board Member

Only Leaders can become Board Members. The Board is elected by all voting-eligible Leaders (meaning Leaders who have active status). Board members are expected to:

  • Carry out their roles to the best of their ability
  • Attend bi-weekly Board meetings
  • Attend and lead the monthly members meeting
  • Engage in club activities

Benefits.

  • Funding (via the Treasurers) to make trip proposals into real trips
  • Leaders’ retreat and Board retreat
  • Gear rental discount
  • Voting rights (for Board and amendment changes)

Board Positions: Roles and Responsibilities

Co-Coordinators (also called Co-Presidents), 2

Year-long position, staggered with one new Co-Coordinator elected each semester.

  • Enacts their general vision for the organization, as supported by the Board
  • Oversees all events in the club, keeping Board members accountable to their positions and providing support where needed
  • Maintains smooth operation of all organizational functions
  • Organizes bi-weekly Board meetings and keeps meeting agendas
  • Manages the club Google Drive and email, yaleoutdoors@gmail.com
  • Completes annual student organization re-registration during the summer
  • Initiates and runs Board elections
  • Plans the semesterly Board retreat
  • Delegates tasks that are not explicitly listed in this constitution
  • Conducts quarterly (mid-semester and end of semester) membership status evaluations. Mid-semester is a quick evaluation to reinstate active Leader membership to those who have previously lost it but have met the requirements. At the end of the semester, all Leaders who have not met the requirements lose their active leadership status and benefits.
  • During transition, ensures all newly elected positions, including Co-Coordinator, have a smooth and fully prepared transition

Treasurers, 2

Year-long position.

  • Manage the club bank account
  • Manage fund transfers through Venmo and Zelle
  • Apply for, submit and deposit all UOFC grants in a timely manner
  • Retain receipts of all expenses to submit for UOFC reconciliation
  • Set and approve budgets for all club functions (Break Trips, kayaking and so on)
  • Reimburse members for group expenses
  • Work with Leaders to coordinate ongoing financial matters, such as financial aid
  • During transition, teach the incoming Treasurers how to apply for UOFC and transfer all bank account information

Expedition Coordinators, 2

Year-long position.

  • Work closely with Weekend Coordinators to make sure all trips are safe and logistically sound
  • Take initiative to take trip ideas and make them happen
  • Focus on large-scale trips, usually outside the state, such as the Fall, Winter and Spring Break backpacking trips. These are the biggest trips and are often to National Parks. This includes encouraging interest in leading, overseeing the formation of trips, and ensuring safety and logistical soundness
  • Stay in communication with all Leaders and the Publicist so the weekly newsletter goes out on time with the correct information
  • Answer “how to run my trip” questions from trip leaders
  • Get Leaders stoked by presenting trip ideas and maintaining the Trip Bible

Weekend Coordinators, 2

Year-long position.

  • Work closely with Expedition Coordinators and share responsibility for making sure trips are safe and logistically sound
  • Take initiative to take trip ideas and make them happen
  • Focus on more local trips, such as day hikes, kayaking and visits to the farm. The Weekend Coordinator is a core pillar in creating consistent events throughout the year
  • Stay in communication with the Publicist so the weekly newsletter goes out on time with the correct information

Gear Room Manager, 1

Year-long position.

  • Manages the YO Gear Room
  • Maintains gear room cleanliness
  • Maintains an updated list of available gear and rental costs, checking in with the Treasurers
  • Oversees gear return, including confirming returned gear is dry and functional and updating the inventory
  • Documents and ensures rented gear is paid for, including rate payments, and that refundable safety deposits are returned
  • Researches and reaches out to local partners to expand the gear room equipment
  • Gets all purchases approved by the Treasurers
  • Spearheads the YO merch and schwag, such as stickers, flasks and t-shirts

Pnucklemaster, 1 to 2

Year-long position.

  • Recruits and trains our future, the Pnuckles (spelled with a “P”)
  • Introduces them to the YO leader community in a positive way, coordinates our presence at the Student Activities Fair in September and Bulldog Days in April, and takes charge of the new leader application process
  • Runs the semesterly Pnuckle orientation and initiation
  • Tracks all Pnuckle progress, making sure all Pnuckles are on track to complete requirements

Publicist, 1

Year-long position.

  • Maintains the Yale Connect website, making sure all pages are updated and all links work
  • Sends the weekly newsletter containing upcoming trips, sign-up links, announcements and relevant community events
  • Maintains the social media presence on Instagram
  • Changes the password upon obtaining accounts, for security, and documents the changes
  • During transition, transfers all social media accounts and teaches the newsletter tool

Social Chairs

Year-long position.

  • Organize at least two YOHOs for YO leaders, and one mixer with other crunchy student organizations, including planning, publicizing, buying food and setting up
  • Organize social events for the wider Yale community and Yale Outdoors members
  • Work with the Weekend Coordinators and Board to plan non-outdoors events
  • Reach out to Harvard and plan the Harvard–Yale crunchyfest

Co-Coordinator Emeriti

Both Co-Coordinators from the previous year are automatically placed in this position. Unlike the rest of the Board, they are not expected to attend all Board meetings and Leaders’ weekly meetings, but remain on the Board panel to provide advice. Their main responsibility is managing institutional knowledge. They are also responsible for alumni coordinating: making sure all graduating Leaders submit post-graduation emails to a centralized document, and coordinating fundraising drives among alumni.

Insufficient Board Members

In the case of a small Board, prioritize having at least one of each position. Co-Coordinators must still be two people.

Reviving dissolved positions

The constitution updated in November 2025 dissolved a few positions: Wilderness Education Chair, Outreach and Community Service Chair, and Events Chair. If future interest and numbers allow, an amendment and an emergency or regular election may revive them.

Elections

Eligibility

All active Leaders may run for Board positions. Co-Coordinators must have at least 1 year of experience on the Board.

Co-Coordinator elections

The Co-Coordinator role is a staggered two-person position in which one person is elected in the fall and one in the spring, each serving a one-year term. Fall Co-Coordinator elections happen along with officer elections, and spring elections happen by the second Saturday of April following the same protocol.

Timeline

  • Towards the end of the fall semester, the Co-Coordinators set an election date, before the second Saturday of December so there is time for transitions
  • Two weeks before, the Co-Coordinators email all eligible Leaders announcing the election. Leaders have one week to fill out an interest form
  • One week before, applications close. The Co-Coordinators compile all candidates into one document and send it to all voting-eligible Leaders
  • On the day, the Co-Coordinators mediate. Candidates may give a short speech. Voting is by anonymous slips. The person with the highest number of votes wins; it does not have to be a majority
  • Within two weeks after, the Co-Coordinators make sure all incumbent and incoming Board members meet and feel confident about their transition

Mid-year vacancy

If a position becomes vacant at the end of the spring term, the Co-Coordinators must hold an emergency election within four weeks, following the guidelines of a regular election. In between, the Co-Coordinators can take on the role or appoint an interim, whose role ends when a new Board member is elected.

Changing the Constitution

The world is an ever-changing place, and YO must change to meet it. The constitution may be changed through an amendment.

  • Leaders and Board members may submit an amendment to the Board via the Co-Coordinators
  • The Co-Coordinators review and present the proposal to the Board within 2 weeks
  • The Board votes on whether to move the proposal to a full vote. A pass requires a simple majority (50% plus one). A quorum is the entire Board
  • If the Board rejects it, the proposal dies, but may be resubmitted
  • If the Board passes it, the Co-Coordinators add it to the docket, a running list of pending proposals. The vote happens at the next election in which a Board position is being voted on, with proposals appearing on the same document as the candidates
  • Only voting-eligible Leaders and Board members may vote. 60% of the voting-eligible people attending the election is enough to pass. There are no quorum requirements
  • If the Leaders reject it, the proposal dies, but may be resubmitted
  • If it passes, it becomes an amendment. Within a week, the Co-Coordinators document it at the bottom of the constitution and change the relevant sections, recording what it was before and what it was changed to
