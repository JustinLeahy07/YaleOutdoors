Join and lead

There is a place for every amount of time you have.

Start here

Getting involved takes two minutes. Join the newsletter, hop in the GroupMe, and come to something. If you like it, the rest is optional.

  1. Join the weekly newsletter. It lists every trip and sign-up link.
  2. Join the GroupMe for quick plans and rides.
  3. Find us on Yale Connect and Instagram.
  4. Come on a trip. Anyone can.
Want to lead?

Pnuckle applications open at the start of every semester. The Pnucklemasters, Carmen Ung and Olivia Bley, run the process.

Apply to pnuckle

Pnuckling is open to undergraduates. Graduate and professional students are welcome on every trip and can rent gear.

The ladder

Yale Outdoors welcomes people at every level of commitment. Climbing is optional, and nobody is behind for staying where they are.

Member

Anyone at Yale

What it takes

  • Get on the mailing list. That is all.

What you get

  • Apply for and join any trip
  • The weekly newsletter and GroupMe
  • Gear rentals

Pnuckle

Undergraduates, any semester

What it takes

  • Apply at the start of a semester
  • Take part in five YO events
  • At least one of those, or an extra one, must be helping to lead or organize an event of any kind
  • Attend initiation

What you get

  • Priority over other members on trips
  • The member rate on gear
  • A path to becoming a Leader

Leader

Has successfully pnuckled

What it takes

  • Finish pnuckling
  • Help lead trips and events
  • Come to club meetings

What you get

  • Priority on trips
  • Wilderness First Aid training
  • Help turning your trip idea into a real trip, with funding through the Treasurers
  • A vote on the Board and on changes to the constitution
  • Leaders’ retreat
  • The member rate on gear

Board

Elected by active Leaders

What it takes

  • Be an active Leader
  • Run for a position and win the vote
  • Meet every two weeks and run a part of the club

What you get

  • Everything Leaders get
  • The Board retreat
  • Your name in the next edition of People

The full rules are in the constitution.

Safety

Trips are planned to be safe before they are fun. Our Safety and Risk Management Policy asks that every trip has:

  • At least one leader certified in Wilderness First Aid and CPR
  • A first aid kit and a first aid manual in the group
  • A written trip proposal reviewed by the coordinators
  • Signed participant waivers
  • Drivers who have taken Yale’s driver safety course

Overnight trips at intermediate or hard levels also need a Leader trained as a Wilderness First Responder.

Questions

Experience?
None needed. Trips are labeled by difficulty and Leaders match them to the group.
Cost?
Trips are priced to cover costs, and some ask for a small deposit. Financial aid is available. No one should miss a trip because of a financial burden, so talk to the Treasurers.
Gear?
You can borrow almost everything from the gear room.
Graduate students?
Welcome on all trips and at the gear room. Only undergraduates can pnuckle.
Who to ask?
Write to yaleoutdoors@gmail.com or see the board.
