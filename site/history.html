History

Nine decades of Yale people going outside.

The short version

  1. 1930s

    An informal “Outing Club” explores Connecticut’s trails and wilderness.

  2. 1936

    The Yale Mountaineering Club is founded, known for climbing-themed antics that included scaling Harkness Tower.

  3. 1947

    The club’s golden age. Strong leaders mount first ascents across the state and North America: the Front Range of northern Colorado, the Brooks Range of Alaska, Mount Wood (15,886 ft) in the St. Elias Mountains, and one of the first documented expeditions into the remote Logan Mountains, which logged nine first ascents and a glaciological survey. Members also catalogued more than eighty routes on Sleeping Giant and published A Climber’s Guide to Ragged Mountain in 1973.

  4. 1970s

    Yale Outdoors is a large organization on campus. The Mountaineering Club’s ambitions become more modest and it is eventually renamed the Yale Climbing Club.

  5. 1990s

    Yale Outdoors is re-founded in 1997. Three registered clubs, the Yale Mountaineering Club, Yale Outdoors and the Forestry Outing Club, reach out to students of every ability.

  6. 2009

    In spring, Yale Outdoors is reorganized as an umbrella organization to bring outdoor life at Yale together and pool resources. A 2009 prospectus makes the case to the university, and a Winter School is proposed. A liaison program brings graduate and professional students into the club the following year.

  7. 2025

    The constitution is rewritten in November. Three Board positions are retired and others adjusted.

  8. 2026

    The constitution is revised again in May. The club runs trips, a gear room, and a Pnuckle program, as it has for decades.

More is in the 2009 Prospectus and the trip report archive.

Giving

Everything we do is run by students, and a few dollars go a long way here. Gifts from alumni and friends go to the club’s account, managed by our Treasurers, and are spent on three things:

  • Transportation. Vans and gas are what it takes to get anyone out of New Haven.
  • Gear. Tents, sleeping bags, boots and boats for the gear room, so rentals stay cheap.
  • Training. Wilderness first aid subsidies for new Leaders, skill sessions and skills-based trips.
