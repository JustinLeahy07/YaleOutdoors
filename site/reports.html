Trip reports

What past trips learned, so the next trip starts ahead.

Write one

After a trip, the Leader writes a short report so the next group knows what to expect: the route, what it cost, what went wrong and what to bring. It takes about fifteen minutes. Fill in the form on this page, or download the template and send it to the Expedition Coordinators.

Destination, dates, weather, leaders, group size, activity, transportation, expenses, route description, maps used, highlights, difficulties, and other comments.

The archive

Nine reports from 2006 to 2009, shortened and with names left out. They are a good picture of what a normal YO trip has always been: a few friends, a couple of cars, and a plan that changes when the weather does.

Jan 2009Winding Trails, cross-country skiing
Where
Winding Trails Recreation Area, Farmington, Connecticut. About 50 minutes from campus.
When
January 25, 2009. Group of 8.
Weather
Cold, sunny and perfect.
Cost
$12 per person trail fee, $15 per person for skis, boots and poles. Gas.

Well-groomed trails through pine forest and easy to follow. The group arrived at 10:30, beat the crowds, and left at 2 when it had filled up. The ski shop opens at 9, so aim earlier. Good conditions are rare and the area is not large, so loops go quickly.

Worth knowing. A good option for beginners because rentals are available.

Nov 2008Macedonia Brook, ridge hike
Where
Macedonia Brook State Park, Connecticut.
When
November 16, 2008. Group of 11.
Weather
Windy, with rain and hail. High 30s to high 40s.
Cost
Gas.

A pleasant walk along the ridge with good views and almost nobody else there. There was some hand-over-hand scrambling, slippery after rain. The group left at 8 and was back by 4:30 at a not-too-quick pace.

Worth knowing. Route from a Trails.com trail guide.

Oct 2008Tyler Mill, mountain biking
Where
Tyler Mill, Wallingford, Connecticut.
When
October 2008. Group of 3.

No injuries or malfunctions, and morale stayed high. The terrain was a little more technical than newer riders expected, though the length of the ride felt right. One rider was near the edge of what his bike could handle.

Worth knowing. The club has no fleet of rental bikes, so riders should check their equipment.

Oct 2008Bear Mountain, Connecticut
Where
Bear Mountain, Salisbury, Connecticut.
When
October 11, 2008. Group of 9.
Weather
Sunny and cloudless.
Cost
About $7 each, for gas, tea and cider.

Started at the Undermountain trail parking lot, took the Paradise Lane bypass to the summit and returned the same way. The drive took about 90 minutes and the climb 3 to 4 hours. Crowded on a perfect Saturday morning.

Worth knowing. Stop at the Chaiwalla tea house in Salisbury. In fall, Ellsworth Farm in nearby Sharon has cider, a corn maze and a pumpkin patch.

Sep 2008Upper Farmington River, canoeing
Where
Upper Farmington River, Connecticut.
When
September 21, 2008. Group of 5.
Weather
Warm and perfect.
Cost
$20 each for the canoe outfitter, plus gas.

Class I to II rapids, with faster water than usual after recent rain. Boats came from Mainstream Canoes and Kayaks. A swim and a picnic on the beach at the end.

Worth knowing. There is a big campground along the river, so camping the night before and paddling the Upper and Lower sections could work. Lunch at the general store by the bridge, about halfway.

Feb 2008Carter–Moriah, snowshoeing
Where
Carter–Moriah Trail, White Mountains, New Hampshire.
When
February 23 to 24, 2008. Group of 5.
Weather
Clearing steadily after three inches of new snow.
Cost
About $170 for gas for two cars, $6 parking.

A storm on Friday night changed the plan. The group left New Haven at 6 am and hiked 4.5 miles up Stony Brook Trail to Imp Campsite, arriving after dark. On Sunday they rose at 4, were snowshoeing by 5, and cut the day short because of loose snow and a very steep climb up North Carter Mountain, coming down 6.5 miles by North Carter and Imp trails. Sunday was a bluebird day with views of the Presidentials, Canada, Maine and even the Atlantic. Used the Map Adventures White Mountains waterproof map.

Worth knowing. Imp Campsite has a shelter and is a good place to start learning winter conditions. Carter–Moriah is often sheltered when the Presidentials get awful weather. Snowshoes were necessary, and crampons would have been if it had been icy. Navigating in the dark is hard in winter, as the snowpack hides the trail.

Oct 2007Long Trail and Camel’s Hump
Where
Long Trail, Green Mountains, Vermont.
When
Mid-October 2007. Group of 5.
Weather
Rain, sleet and fog.
Cost
Gas and $5 each for the cabin.

18.5 miles between Appalachian Gap and the Winooski River, including Camel’s Hump, with a night in a Green Mountain Club cabin at about the midpoint. Two cars, with one left at each end. Fall color was at its peak, with long views to Lake Champlain and the Adirondacks, and old plane wreckage below the summit. Ice near the top forced the group to circle back on trails below treeline.

Worth knowing. Wet slab rock is slick. The drive is over four hours each way. Used the Wilderness Map Company waterproof Long Trail map.

Sep 2007Presidential Traverse
Where
Presidential Range, New Hampshire.
When
September 19 to 21, 2007. Group of 6.
Weather
Windy and cold: high 30s on the ridge, 70s lower down.
Cost
Gas, $10 each for shelter fees, extra food.

Arrive at Pinkham Notch around 6 or 7 pm and hike 2 miles in the dark to the Hermit Lake shelter. The next morning, a full day of backpacking over Mount Washington, Jefferson and Madison, with the option of Adams, then down to a night on the platforms at Osgood Tentsite. Hike the remaining 4 miles out on Sunday and be back on campus by early evening. Used the AMC White Mountain Guide.

Worth knowing. A great two-to-three-day backpacking route, especially for YO. Very windy and cold at the top.

Nov 2006White Mountains, three peaks
Where
Mount Washington, Mount Jefferson and the Franconia loop, New Hampshire.
When
Late November 2006. Group of 3.
Weather
Alternating cloudy and sunny.
Cost
Food.

Dayhikes from a base at Dolly Copp campground near Pinkham Notch: up Mount Washington by the Lion Head route, Mount Jefferson by Caps Ridge, and a Franconia loop over Lafayette and Lincoln. Reaching the top of Jefferson in a cloud inversion, with cloud banks rolling over the Presidential summits to reveal views, was the highlight. The last half mile above treeline was near whiteout, and there was ice on Lion Head.

Worth knowing. One 2WD car was enough. Used the Map Adventures White Mountains waterproof map.
